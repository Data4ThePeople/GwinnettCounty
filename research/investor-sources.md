# Investor and institutional ownership of single-family homes: sources for Gwinnett and metro Atlanta

Compiled October 21, 2026. One entry per source. Each figure lists the quote or table value, where it appears, the geography, the years, and the source's definition of "investor" or "institutional."

Status labels:
- VERIFIED: read in the primary document.
- SECONDHAND: seen only in a summary, news story, or press release, not in the primary document.

Key caution up front: sources use very different definitions (1+ non-owner-occupied homes, LLC name keywords, 15+ homes, 50+ homes, 100+ homes, 1,000+ homes, or a named list of 3 or 7 firms) and different denominators (all single-family homes, single-family rentals, all rentals, or all sales). Numbers from different entries are not comparable unless the definitions match.

Gwinnett-specific figures found, in brief:
- ARC (Parcl Labs, seven named firms, published Nov. 2024): 9,680 homes in Gwinnett, the most of any county in the 21-county region; 3.8% of Gwinnett single-family units. (VERIFIED, entry 3)
- Raymond / Georgia Tech (ZTRAX, Gwinnett and DeKalb combined, spring 2019 to summer 2021): large corporate buyers made 17% of single-family sales and 53% of single-family rental purchases by summer 2021. Not split by county. (VERIFIED in the Atlanta Studies summary, entry 4)
- BatchData (commercial, Q4 2025): 47,085 investor-owned single-family properties in Gwinnett, 17.5% of 269,665. 1,000+ tier holds 8,793. (VERIFIED on the vendor page, entry 10)
- No source found gives a Gwinnett figure for 2005 to 2009, or a Gwinnett time series covering both 2005-2009 and 2020-2024.

## 1. GAO, Rental Housing: Information on Institutional Investment in Single-Family Homes (GAO-24-106643)

- Publisher: U.S. Government Accountability Office. Report to congressional committees.
- Date: May 2024 (product page says May 22, 2024).
- URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106643 ; PDF https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-106643.pdf
- Status: VERIFIED (full PDF text read).
- Geography: Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Alpharetta metro area. No county figures. Gwinnett is not mentioned anywhere in the report.
- Definition: "Most market trends analyses we reviewed defined institutional investors as those owning 1,000 or more properties." (p. 13). For the literature review, "institutional investor" means "large, single-family rental investors, which may be defined differently by study authors" (p. 17).

Figures:
- p. 15: "as of June 2022, regions with the heaviest concentration of institutional investment were Atlanta, Jacksonville, and Charlotte. In these markets, institutional investor-owned homes constituted about 25 percent, 21 percent, and 18 percent of the single-family rental market, respectively." This is GAO's own analysis of the Urban Institute data (entry 2). Years: numerator 2022, denominator 2021 ACS.
- p. 15, footnote 40: "institutional investors owned almost 72,000 single-family rental homes in Atlanta, Ga.; 33,000 homes in Phoenix, Ariz.; and 27,000 homes in Dallas, Tex., as of 2022." Cited to Goodman et al. (Urban Institute).
- p. 15, footnote 40 and p. 16, Figure 6 note: GAO says the shares "may be overstated" because the numerator includes vacant homes while the denominator excludes them, and because the numerator is 2022 data while the denominator is 2021 data.
- p. 16, Figure 6 title: "Estimated Share of the Single-Family Rental Market Held by Investors with over 1,000 Homes in Selected Areas, as of 2022." The figure is a map; the 25 percent Atlanta value comes from the text on p. 15.
- p. 14: "Atlanta, Dallas, Charlotte, and Houston were among the cities with the largest number of single-family rental homes owned by investors with more than 1,000 homes, as of 2022."
- p. 17: describes a study of the Atlanta metro (Charles 2020, four largest single-family rental REITs as of December 2018) that found different firms bought in neighborhoods with very different racial makeups.

On rents (pp. 20 to 21), relevant to the post's question: GAO reports mixed findings. "Some studies we reviewed found institutional investors may have contributed to an increase in rents after the 2007-2009 financial crisis, particularly in locations with concentrated institutional investor activity." Then: "However, other studies found more limited effects of investors on rents." One study (Mills et al., eight largest investors, 2012 to 2014) found a larger investor share of purchases in a zip code "was not highly correlated with subsequent changes in rents." Another (Lambie-Hanson et al.) found an association with county-level rents from 2011 through 2014 that "was not statistically significant." p. 17: "few of the studies assessed the effect of institutional investors after 2017."

Note on use: Sen. Ossoff's May 2025 letters cite this report for the claim that "institutional investors now hold a 25% share of the single-family rental market in metro Atlanta" (VERIFIED in the letter, entry 12). The AJC (May 14, 2025) reports Ossoff describing the 25% as homes "owned by out-of-state companies," which is not how GAO defines it.

## 2. Urban Institute, A Profile of Institutional Investor-Owned Single-Family Rental Properties

- Authors: Laurie Goodman, Amalie Zinn, Kathryn Reynolds, Owen Noble.
- Publisher: Urban Institute, Housing Finance Policy Center.
- Date: April 2023 (corrected April 25, 2023).
- URL: https://www.urban.org/research/publication/profile-institutional-investor-owned-single-family-rental-properties ; PDF https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2023-08/A%20Profile%20of%20Institutional%20Investor%E2%80%93Owned%20Single-Family%20Rental%20Properties.pdf
- Status: VERIFIED (full PDF text read).
- Geography: Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Alpharetta MSA. No county figures.
- Data: "a national property records database," with all legal entities of a parent company combined. Holdings as of June 2022.
- Definition: "We have defined an institutional investor as an entity that owns at least 100 single-family homes" (p. 1). "Mega" investors own more than 1,000 properties; "smaller" own more than 100 but less than 1,000; "local" are a separate group of long-term holders. The correction note says the cutoff was changed from 25 to 100 properties.

Figures:
- p. 7: "Atlanta has 72,000 mega operator-owned properties, Phoenix has 33,000, Dallas has 27,000."
- Appendix Table A.1 (p. 22), Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Alpharetta: All 82,327; Mega 71,832; Small 1,846; Local 1,912. (All institutional investor-owned single-family properties, 2022.)
- Table 2 (p. 11), Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Alpharetta: institutional SFR share of all rental properties 10.0%; institutional SFR share of SFR properties 28.6%; single-family share of all rental properties 35.1%. Text p. 10: "In Atlanta, the largest market, SFR operators own 10 percent of all rental properties and 28.6 percent of all SFR properties." The report says these shares are "slightly overstated" for the same vacancy and 2021-vs-2022 reasons GAO gives.
- p. 7, on timing: "Recent CoreLogic research shows that institutional investors tend to select markets where rents are rising quickly. That is, investor purchases tend to lag, not lead rent increases."
- p. 2 notes that "Other research focused on the Atlanta region classifies an institutional investor as one who owns at least 15 properties (Raymond et al. 2016)."

Reconciling with GAO: Urban's 28.6% uses the 100+ definition. GAO's "about 25 percent" is GAO's own calculation for 1,000+ owners from the same data.

## 4. Elora Lee Raymond (Georgia Tech), Large Corporate Investors purchase thousands of Atlanta Single-Family Homes during the Pandemic

- Publisher: Atlanta Studies (Atlanta Studies Network). DOI https://doi.org/10.18737/atls20220128
- Date: January 28, 2022.
- URL: https://atlantastudies.org/2022/01/28/large-corporate-investors-purchase-thousands-of-atlanta-single-family-homes-during-the-pandemic/ (read via the Internet Archive copy, since the live site blocked automated access)
- Status: VERIFIED for this blog summary. The underlying brief was not read (see "Not found").
- Underlying brief: Raymond, E.L., Zha, Y., Knight-Scott, E., and Cabrera, L. (2022). "Large corporate buyers of residential rental housing during the COVID19 pandemic in three southeastern cities." Planning + Property Lab at Georgia Tech, Research Brief 2022-1. Funded by the Urban Institute / Housing Crisis Research Collaborative.
- Geography: Gwinnett and DeKalb counties combined. The post calls this "Atlanta," but says "the focus of the study is restricted to Gwinnett and DeKalb counties." Footnote 2: "Because the buyers for this research were hand-coded by GRAs, we restricted the sample to two counties where corporate buyers have historically been heavily invested." Sales figures are not split by county.
- Years: spring 2019 to summer 2021 (five quarters before the pandemic, five quarters after).
- Data: Zillow ZTRAX.
- Definition: "large corporate buyers" are hand-coded named firms in three types (Table 3): trading platforms (Opendoor, Zillow, Offerpad, HomeVestors), rent-to-own (Divvy Homes), and institutional SFR (Pretium Partners, Sylvan Road, American Homes 4 Rent, BNY Mellon / Amherst Capital, Blackstone, Cerberus Capital Management, Starwood Capital Group). Not a size threshold.

Figures:
- "In summer of 2021, large corporate investors bought 17% of all single-family homes that were sold in Atlanta."
- "By summer 2021, large corporate investor purchases represented 53% of all single-family rentals, and 17% of all single-family home sales." ("single-family rentals" here means purchases of single-family homes that became rentals.)
- Table 1, "All Purchases by Housing Type" (Pre-Pandemic / Pandemic / % change / Q2 2021): Single-family Rental 9,084 / 8,259 / -9% / 2,795. Large Corporate Buyers 3,387 / 3,501 / 3% / 1,494. Owner Occupied 32,823 / 33,734 / 3% / 7,374.
- "by Q2 2021, more than doubled the average quarterly transaction volumes of 2019" (large corporate buyers).
- "Between January 2019 and June 2021, prices increased 51% in Gwinnett and 78% in DeKalb." (Figure 2, "Single-family Rentals, Median Sales Price by County.") This is the one county-specific number in the piece.
- Table 2, median purchase price, large corporate SFR firms: $132,500 (2019 Q2), $275,000 (2021 Q2), 108% change, CAGR 28%. Households/owner-occupied: $260,000 to $335,200, 29%, CAGR 9%. The authors caution: "we do not control for market tier or the composition of homes sold, so any firm conclusions about the contribution of large corporate buyers to rising prices would require further analysis."

Related: Raymond's House testimony (entry 7) repeats the 53% and 17% as "In Atlanta alone" without noting the two-county scope, and describes the 28% as "every quarter" where the blog calls it an annual rate.

## 15. Brian Y. An (Georgia Tech), The Influence of Institutional Single-Family Rental Investors on Homeownership: Who Gets Targeted and Pushed Out of the Local Market?

- Journal of Planning Education and Research, published online June 2023. DOI 10.1177/0739456X231176072. SSRN abstract 4156851.
- Status: SECONDHAND (Georgia Tech news release, August 7, 2023, https://news.gatech.edu/news/2023/08/07/investors-force-black-families-out-home-ownership-new-research-shows , and the AJC). Full paper not read.
- Geography: Atlanta metro, about 800 neighborhoods (census tracts per AELP's description: Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell MSA). No county figures found.
- Years: 2007 to 2016.
- Definition: per AELP's summary of the paper, large corporate investors that bought at least 50 properties in any year; investors that bought 49 or fewer had no measurable effect.
- Figures as reported: investor purchases rose "from nearly 0% in 2007 to over 12% in the peak year, 2013" (average neighborhood); "Investors acquired up to 76% of for-sale, single-family homes in some neighborhoods"; AJC reports "a 1.4 percentage point drop in homeownership rates in metro Atlanta from 2007 to 2016," "16,500 fewer households," and "a 4.2 percentage point drop in Black homeownership." Black households "lost more than $4 billion in home equity over a 10-year period."
