## 3. Body, part one

```
Last week, we dug into our Rentals Within Reach visualization and pulled out what looked like one of the most affordable counties in America for renters. That was Dawson County, Nebraska, and while it’s true that it used to be affordable, that all changed earlier this year when its largest employer – the Tyson meatpacking plant – shut its doors.

Today, we went hunting on the opposite side of the ledger – the counties that were the least affordable for renters. Interestingly, three of the ten least affordable counties outside college counties were in the Atlanta metro area. But one county stood out from the rest – Gwinnett County, situated northeast of downtown Atlanta. Gwinnett County had just 10.5% of rental units “within reach” of the median renter household income. We define within reach as a rent of no more than 30% of the median renter household’s after-tax income.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Gwinnett's 10.5% was the lowest of the 1,922 counties we ranked, leaving out college counties. In 2005-2009, it was 25.5%. Over those years, after inflation, Gwinnett's median rent rose 32% while its median renter household income rose 8%.

Here is the twist. Gwinnett added about 41,000 rentals over this period, yet it lost about 5,500 rentals within reach. And the pay of renters who worked full time all year showed no measurable rise.

So how does a county add that many rentals and still end up with fewer that its renters can afford? The full report walks through where the new rentals came in, what happened to the older apartments that used to be close to reach, and how Gwinnett's renter pay compares with other counties across the country where rent rose 25% or more.

Gwinnett is an extreme example, but rents are outpacing renter incomes in other places too, so it works as a case study for the rest of America. When rents outpace renter incomes year after year, it can work like a gravitational pull, widening the gap between the economic prospects of renters and homeowners and putting the American Dream further out of reach for renters.
```
